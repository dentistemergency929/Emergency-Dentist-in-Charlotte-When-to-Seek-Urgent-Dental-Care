## What Are the Most Common Dental Emergencies?

Dental emergencies can affect people of all ages. Some develop gradually, while others happen suddenly following an accident or injury.

Common problems that may require urgent dental assessment include:

- **Severe toothache:** Persistent or throbbing pain may indicate deep decay, inflammation or an infection inside the tooth.
- **Broken or cracked teeth:** A fracture can expose sensitive tooth tissue and cause pain when eating or drinking.
- **Dental abscesses:** An infection may cause swelling, pus, a bad taste in the mouth or tenderness around the affected tooth.
- **Knocked-out teeth:** A permanent tooth that has been completely displaced needs immediate attention.
- **Lost fillings or crowns:** A damaged restoration can expose the underlying tooth and increase sensitivity.
- **Bleeding or soft-tissue injuries:** Cuts to the gums, lips or tongue may require treatment if bleeding persists.

Not every dental problem requires an extraction or a complicated procedure. A dentist will examine the affected area and determine whether the tooth can be restored or needs another form of treatment.

## When Should You See a Dentist Immediately?

Pain that persists, swelling around the jaw, pain when biting and sensitivity that interferes with daily activities are all reasons to seek dental advice promptly. Fever, pus around the gums or swelling that continues to increase may indicate an infection requiring urgent assessment.

A toothache that temporarily disappears should not automatically be considered resolved. The underlying cause may still need treatment, particularly if decay or infection has reached the inner part of the tooth.

Some symptoms require hospital care rather than a routine dental appointment. Difficulty breathing or swallowing, rapidly spreading facial or neck swelling, or signs that an infection is threatening the airway require immediate emergency medical attention.

## What Treatments Can Help With a Dental Emergency?

The appropriate treatment depends on the cause of the problem, the extent of the damage and the patient's oral health.

**Tooth decay and damaged teeth:** Small areas of decay may be treated with fillings. More extensive damage may require a crown or root canal treatment if the tooth can be preserved.

**Dental infections:** An abscess may need to be drained, while treatment of the affected tooth may involve root canal therapy or extraction. Antibiotics may be prescribed in certain circumstances, but they do not replace treatment of the source of the infection.

**Broken teeth:** Depending on the fracture, a dentist may repair the tooth with bonding material, a filling or a crown. Severe damage may require removal.

**Knocked-out permanent teeth:** Time is important. Handle the tooth by its crown, not its root, and avoid scrubbing it. Keep it moist in milk or a suitable tooth-preservation solution while seeking immediate dental advice. Do not force the tooth back into its socket.

An examination helps establish which option is appropriate and whether additional treatment is needed to protect the surrounding teeth and gums.

## How Can You Manage Tooth Pain Before Your Appointment?

If you cannot receive immediate treatment, a few simple measures may help control discomfort temporarily.

- Rinse gently with warm salt water if you can do so comfortably.
- Place a cold compress against the outside of your cheek to help reduce swelling.
- Eat soft foods and avoid chewing on the affected side.
- Use over-the-counter pain relief according to the label, provided it is safe for you.
- Keep the area clean and avoid pressing sharp objects against the tooth or gums.

Do not place aspirin directly on the affected area, as it can damage the soft tissues. Avoid trying to drain an abscess yourself or using leftover antibiotics without professional advice.

These measures may provide temporary relief, but they cannot repair a fracture or reliably resolve an infection. If symptoms worsen, seek prompt professional assessment.
